# Tax on Holiday Rental Income: What Every Host Must Know in 2026

The first question every new holiday rental host asks me is: "Do I have to pay tax on this income?" The answer is almost always yes—but the more important question is: "How much, and how do I legally minimize it?"

After 32 years in the STR and holiday rental industry, I can tell you that the hosts who are genuinely anxious about their tax bill typically haven't done the work to understand what they can deduct. When they see the gross rental income number, they assume the tax will be brutal. When we actually work through their legitimate deductions together, the picture looks radically different.

This guide covers how holiday rental income is taxed across the major markets—US, UK, EU member states, and Australia—and the strategies available to reduce that tax burden legally.

---

## Is Holiday Rental Income Taxable?

Yes, with one narrow exception in the United States (the Augusta Rule—discussed below), holiday rental income is taxable. It doesn't matter whether the income comes through Airbnb, Vrbo, Booking.com, or a direct booking. It doesn't matter whether you receive it in cash, bank transfer, or PayPal. It doesn't matter whether you only rented for a few weeks.

If you received money in exchange for allowing someone to stay at your property, that money is income and must be reported.

The critical variables that determine *how* it's taxed and *how much* you pay are:
- Which country (and state/region) the property is located in
- Whether the property is your primary or secondary residence
- How many days per year you rent vs. personally use it
- What deductible expenses you can offset against the income

## United States: How the IRS Taxes Holiday Rental Income

### The Augusta Rule: The One Tax-Free Exception

Under IRC Section 280A(g), if you rent your home for **14 days or fewer** per year, the rental income is completely excluded from taxable income. You don't report it. You don't pay tax on it.

This provision is absolute: it doesn't phase out with income, it doesn't require any filing, and there are no strings attached—other than strictly staying under the 15-day threshold.

For hosts in highly desirable locations (ski resort towns, coastal destinations, cities during major events), this can translate to tens of thousands in tax-free income annually. A host near Augusta, Georgia who rents during Masters Week, or a Nantucket homeowner who rents for two summer weeks at $3,000/night, collects tax-free income under this rule.

The moment you rent a 15th day, however, the exception evaporates entirely. All income from day 1 becomes taxable.

### For 15+ Days: Schedule E Reporting

If you rent your holiday property for 15 or more days per year, you report the income and deduct expenses on **Schedule E (Supplemental Income and Loss)**.

Your tax treatment then depends on your personal use:

**If personal use ≤ 14 days AND ≤ 10% of rental days:**
Property is treated as a rental business. All rental expenses are deductible. Rental losses may offset other income (subject to passive activity rules).

**If personal use > 14 days OR > 10% of rental days:**
Property is treated as a "vacation home." Expenses are deductible only in proportion to rental days. Losses cannot offset other income.

### Federal Income Tax Rates on Holiday Rental Income

Holiday rental income is taxed at your ordinary marginal income tax rate—the same rate as your wages. The 2026 federal brackets are:

| Income Range (Single) | Tax Rate |
|----------------------|----------|
| $0 – $11,925 | 10% |
| $11,926 – $48,475 | 12% |
| $48,476 – $103,350 | 22% |
| $103,351 – $197,300 | 24% |
| $197,301 – $250,525 | 32% |
| $250,526 – $626,350 | 35% |
| Over $626,350 | 37% |

For most active STR hosts, rental income is stacked on top of other income, pushing marginal rates to 22%, 24%, or 32%. This is why deductions are so critical—every $10,000 in legitimate deductions saves you $2,200–$3,200 in federal tax.

### State Income Tax on Holiday Rental Income

State income tax on rental income varies dramatically:

- **No state income tax:** Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming
- **Flat tax states:** Colorado (4.4%), Illinois (4.95%), Michigan (4.05%), Pennsylvania (3.07%)
- **High-tax progressive states:** California (up to 13.3%), Hawaii (up to 11%), New Jersey (up to 10.75%), Oregon (up to 9.9%)

If you own a holiday rental in California and are a California resident, your combined federal + state marginal rate on that rental income could approach 50%.

### Local Lodging Tax (Transient Occupancy Tax)

In addition to income taxes, US holiday rental hosts typically must collect and remit local Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) from guests. TOT rates commonly range from 6% to 18%.

This is not a tax on your profit—it's a tax collected from guests that you pass through to the government. But if you fail to collect or remit it, the liability becomes yours personally.

## United Kingdom: Tax on Holiday Rental Income

### Post-April 2025: FHL Regime Abolished

The most significant recent change in UK holiday rental taxation is the abolition of the Furnished Holiday Letting (FHL) regime from April 6, 2025.

Under the old FHL rules, qualifying holiday lets received significant tax advantages: capital allowances on furniture and equipment, full mortgage interest deductibility, and Business Asset Disposal Relief on sale. All of these advantages have now been removed.

From April 2025, UK holiday rental income is taxed under the standard residential lettings framework:

### How UK Holiday Rental Income Is Now Taxed

**Income Tax:**
Holiday rental income is subject to UK income tax at your marginal rate: 20% (basic), 40% (higher), or 45% (additional). You report it on your Self Assessment tax return each year.

**Allowable Deductions (Current Rules):**
- Mortgage interest: Limited to 20% tax credit (not full deduction)
- Property maintenance and repairs: Deductible at rental percentage
- Management fees: 100% deductible
- Insurance: Deductible at rental percentage
- Cleaning: 100% deductible for direct rental costs
- Advertising: 100% deductible

**Property Income Allowance:**
If your total property income is £1,000 or less, you can claim the £1,000 property income allowance instead of deducting actual expenses. If your gross income exceeds £1,000, you claim actual expenses instead.

**Capital Gains Tax on Sale:**
When you sell a UK holiday rental property, CGT applies:
- Basic rate taxpayers: 18% on residential property gains
- Higher/additional rate taxpayers: 24% on residential property gains
- You must report and pay CGT within 60 days of completion
- Business Asset Disposal Relief (10% rate) is no longer available for former FHL properties for disposals from April 2025 onwards

## European Union: Tax on Holiday Rental Income

### France

In France, holiday rental income (revenus BIC — bénéfices industriels et commerciaux) is taxed under one of two regimes:

**Micro-BIC (Revenue under €77,700):**
- 50% flat-rate deduction on gross revenue
- Tax applies to the remaining 50%
- Simple reporting—no need to track individual expenses
- Plus 17.2% social charges on the same taxable base

**Régime Réel (Revenue over €77,700 or by election):**
- Deduct actual expenses including depreciation
- More complex but potentially more tax-efficient
- Requires detailed bookkeeping

**Critical requirement:** Hosts must register with the local authority (mairie) before listing. Paris, Lyon, and Nice have implemented quotas and mandatory permit numbers that must appear on listings.

### Spain

Non-resident holiday rental income in Spain is taxed under the Impuesto sobre la Renta de No Residentes (IRNR):
- EU/EEA residents: 19% on net rental income (expenses deductible)
- Non-EU/EEA residents: 24% on gross rental income (no expense deductions)

Spanish residents declare holiday rental income on their general IRPF (income tax) return and benefit from a 60% reduction on net rental income for long-term lets—but this reduction does not apply to tourist/holiday rentals.

Spain has dramatically tightened tourist license requirements. In many regions (Catalonia, Balearic Islands, Canary Islands, Madrid), new licenses for entire-home holiday rentals are being frozen or strictly limited. Operating without a valid tourist license carries fines up to €60,000 per property.

### Portugal

Portugal taxes non-resident holiday rental income at a flat 25% rate. Residents include rental income in their general income tax return and may benefit from certain deductions.

The NHR (Non-Habitual Resident) program, which offered favorable tax treatment for foreign income, was significantly reformed from January 2024 (replaced by the "IFICI" regime with different qualification criteria). New arrivals should not assume the old NHR tax advantages apply.

### Germany

German holiday rental income (Einkünfte aus Vermietung und Verpachtung) is taxed at your progressive income tax rate. The property must demonstrate commercial intent: evidence includes active marketing, professional management, and consistent availability to the general public. If HMRC classifies the activity as a "Liebhaberei" (hobby), losses cannot be offset against other income.

## Australia: Tax on Holiday Rental Income

In Australia, holiday rental income is assessable income for tax purposes and reported in your individual income tax return.

### How Australian Holiday Rental Tax Works

All rental income is included in your assessable income and taxed at your marginal rate. Australian marginal rates range from 19% to 45% (plus 2% Medicare Levy).

**Key deductibility rules:**
- The property must be "genuinely available for rent" to claim full deductions during non-rental periods
- If the property is genuinely available but simply not booked, you can still deduct expenses for those periods
- If you use the property personally, you must apportion expenses between private and income-producing use

**The ATO scrutinizes:**
1. **Unreasonably high prices** that prevent genuine renting (pricing the property above market just to keep it "available")
2. **Restricted availability** (only listing certain seasons, blocking family weekends)
3. **Excessive personal use** without proper apportionment

The ATO has an active compliance program targeting holiday home deductions and conducts regular data-matching exercises using booking platform data.

**Capital Gains Tax:**
Australian holiday homes are subject to CGT on disposal. The 50% CGT discount applies if the property is held for more than 12 months. The discount does not apply to the proportion of the property's use that was for income-producing purposes (this is an area of complexity requiring specialist advice).

### GST on Australian Holiday Rentals

In Australia, short-term accommodation is a taxable supply for GST purposes. If your total annual GST turnover (including holiday rental income) exceeds $75,000, you must register for GST, charge 10% GST on rental receipts, and lodge regular BAS statements.

Many holiday rental hosts breach the $75,000 threshold without realizing it—especially in premium markets.

## Cross-Border Tax Issues: Owning Holiday Rentals Abroad

If you're a US, UK, or Australian resident who owns holiday rental property in another country, you face cross-border tax complexity:

### Double Taxation

Both your home country and the country where the property is located have the right to tax the rental income. To prevent true double taxation, most countries have bilateral tax treaties that provide for:
- Taxation in the source country (where the property is)
- Credit or exemption in the residence country for tax already paid

**US example:** A US resident with a French holiday rental pays French tax on the income. They report the same income on their US return but claim a Foreign Tax Credit for the French tax paid. If the French tax rate is higher than the equivalent US rate on that income, no additional US tax is owed.

### Reporting Requirements

US citizens and residents must report all worldwide income on their US tax returns, regardless of where the property is located. Additional reporting may be required:
- FBAR (FinCEN Form 114): If foreign bank accounts hold the rental proceeds and the aggregate exceeds $10,000 at any point in the year
- Form 8938 (FATCA): For specified foreign financial assets above certain thresholds

Failure to file these forms carries severe penalties ($10,000+ per violation) that can dwarf the underlying tax owed. If you own foreign holiday rental property, get specialized advice.

## Tax Planning Strategies That Reduce Holiday Rental Tax

### Strategy 1: Maximize Deductions Every Year

The single biggest lever is claiming every legitimate deduction: depreciation (including cost segregation), mortgage interest, insurance, cleaning, management fees, advertising, repairs, and professional services. Hosts who work with specialist CPAs consistently pay 30–50% less tax than those who don't.

### Strategy 2: Manage Personal Use Days

Your personal use days determine which tax regime applies (vacation home vs. rental property) and the size of your expense allocation. Even small reductions in personal use days can shift your classification to the more favorable rental property treatment.

Days spent on the property for legitimate maintenance and repairs do **not** count as personal use. If you spend a week doing repairs rather than vacationing, that week doesn't count against you.

### Strategy 3: The 1031 Exchange (US)

When selling a US rental property, a 1031 exchange allows you to defer both capital gains tax and depreciation recapture by reinvesting the proceeds into another "like-kind" property. This can defer millions in tax liability indefinitely—until final sale or death (when the step-up in basis eliminates it entirely).

### Strategy 4: Timing Income and Expenses

By timing bookings and expenses strategically, you can shift income and deductions between tax years. Accelerating deductible expenses into a high-income year or deferring income to a lower-income year can reduce your overall tax bill.

### Strategy 5: Business Entity Structuring

For hosts with significant rental portfolios, operating through an LLC, S-Corp, or other entity may provide tax advantages. This is a complex area requiring specialist advice—the right structure depends on your jurisdiction, portfolio size, and personal tax situation.

## FAQs: Tax on Holiday Rental Income

**Q: What if I don't receive a tax form from Airbnb—do I still need to report the income?**
A: Yes. Tax reporting obligations exist regardless of whether you receive a form. The IRS requires reporting of all income, and "I didn't receive a 1099-K" is not a defense in an audit. As of 2026, the 1099-K threshold is $600—virtually all active hosts will receive one.

**Q: I rented my home through a neighbor, not a platform. Does that still count?**
A: Absolutely. All rental income is taxable regardless of how it's transacted. Off-platform income is harder for tax authorities to trace, but it's no less obligatory to report. And as tax authorities develop more sophisticated data-matching tools, the risk of detection for cash rentals is increasing.

**Q: Can I claim the home office deduction for managing my holiday rental?**
A: If you have a dedicated space used exclusively and regularly for managing your rental business, yes. The home office deduction can cover a proportional share of your home expenses. The "exclusive use" requirement is strictly enforced—a shared space doesn't qualify.

**Q: How is holiday rental income treated differently from regular rental income?**
A: The fundamental difference is personal use. Regular residential rentals typically involve no personal use by the owner. Holiday rentals involve significant owner use, triggering the vacation home rules that limit deductions and restrict loss offsets. The number of days you personally use the property is the critical variable.
