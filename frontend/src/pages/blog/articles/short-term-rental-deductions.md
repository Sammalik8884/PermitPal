# Short-Term Rental Deductions: Every Write-Off You're Entitled to in 2026

I have spent 32 years helping short-term rental operators reduce their tax bills legally and aggressively. The single most consistent finding across every host I've ever worked with—from a couple renting their beach cabin two months a year to a professional property manager running 50 units—is this: **most hosts dramatically underestimate their deductions.**

They know about mortgage interest and property taxes. A few know about depreciation. Almost none know about the full spectrum of legitimate write-offs that the tax code provides.

This guide is comprehensive. Every deduction category I cover is legitimate, well-established in the IRS code, and routinely taken by sophisticated STR operators. If you're not taking all of them, you're overpaying.

---

## Before We Begin: The Two Rules That Govern Everything

### Rule 1: Ordinary and Necessary

To be deductible, an expense must be "ordinary and necessary" in the operation of your rental business. Ordinary means common in the rental industry. Necessary means helpful and appropriate. This is a low bar—nearly every cost you incur running your STR meets it.

### Rule 2: Personal vs. Rental Allocation

When an expense serves both your personal enjoyment of the property and the rental activity, only the rental portion is deductible. The rental percentage is calculated as:

**Rental days ÷ (Rental days + Personal use days) = Rental %**

Expenses that are *exclusively* for the rental (cleaning between guest stays, platform fees, guest supplies) are 100% deductible. Shared expenses (mortgage interest, utilities, insurance) are deductible at the rental percentage.

With these two rules clear, let's go through every deduction you're entitled to.

## Category A: The Big Four (Highest Dollar Value)

### Deduction 1: Depreciation

**Estimated annual value: $8,000–$25,000+ per property**

Depreciation is the non-cash annual deduction for the gradual wear and tear on your rental property. You don't spend any money—the deduction is calculated mathematically—but it reduces your taxable rental income by thousands every year.

**The mechanics:**
- Residential rental properties depreciate over 27.5 years using the straight-line method
- Only the building (not the land) is depreciable
- Your depreciable basis = purchase price + closing costs + improvements, minus land value
- If the property has mixed personal/rental use, apply the rental percentage to the depreciable basis

**Formula:** Depreciable basis × Rental % ÷ 27.5 = Annual depreciation

*Example:* $450,000 property (land value $80,000) = $370,000 building value. 100% rental use. Annual depreciation = $370,000 ÷ 27.5 = **$13,455 per year.**

Over a decade, that's $134,550 in deductions from a property you haven't spent a single additional dollar maintaining.

**What to watch for:** When you sell the property, the IRS "recaptures" the depreciation you claimed at a 25% rate. But you're still better off taking the deduction now at your marginal rate (22–37%) and paying 25% later. The time value of money advantage is significant.

### Deduction 2: Cost Segregation (Accelerated Depreciation)

**Estimated first-year value: $15,000–$90,000**

A cost segregation study is an engineering analysis that identifies which components of your property qualify for accelerated depreciation: 5-year or 15-year property rather than 27.5-year.

Under the 2026 bonus depreciation schedule (60%), these components can be partially deducted in year one.

**Components typically identified:**
- Carpeting, vinyl, and specialty flooring: 5-year
- Appliances (refrigerators, dishwashers, washers/dryers): 5-year
- Decorative lighting and ceiling fans: 5-year
- Countertops and cabinetry (if not integral to building structure): 5-year
- Landscaping, driveways, fencing, parking areas: 15-year
- Outdoor lighting: 15-year

**ROI example:** A $600,000 STR property. Cost segregation identifies $120,000 in 5-year/15-year assets. At 60% bonus depreciation = $72,000 in additional first-year deductions. At 32% tax rate = **$23,040 in year-one federal tax savings.** Study cost: $5,000–$8,000.

Every STR property worth more than $250,000 should have a cost segregation study in the first year of rental use.

### Deduction 3: Mortgage Interest

**Estimated annual value: $8,000–$35,000**

Interest paid on a mortgage secured by the rental property is fully deductible as a rental expense (at the rental percentage if mixed use). Unlike personal mortgage interest, there is no dollar cap on rental property mortgage interest.

**Important nuances:**
- Points paid to obtain the mortgage must be amortized over the loan term (not deducted in full in year one)
- Refinance points are also amortized over the new loan term
- Home equity loan interest is only deductible as rental expense if the proceeds were used to improve the rental property

If you're taking the standard deduction personally (not itemizing), you still get the full rental mortgage interest deduction as a rental expense on Schedule E. These are separate deductions.

### Deduction 4: Property Taxes

**Estimated annual value: $2,000–$15,000**

The rental percentage of your property tax bill is deductible as a rental expense—with no SALT cap. This is critically important: while the $10,000 SALT cap limits your personal property tax deduction on Schedule A, the rental portion of the same tax is fully deductible on Schedule E.

*Example:* $12,000 annual property tax. 80% rental use = $9,600 deductible on Schedule E. The remaining $2,400 personal portion goes on Schedule A (subject to SALT cap).

## Category B: Operating Expenses (100% Deductible Direct Costs)

These expenses relate exclusively to the rental activity and are fully deductible regardless of personal use.

### Platform and Channel Fees

Every dollar you pay to booking platforms is a deductible advertising/commission expense:
- Airbnb host service fee (~3% of booking subtotal)
- Vrbo commission (5–8%) or annual subscription (~$499)
- Booking.com commission (~15%)
- Direct booking website hosting, domain registration, maintenance
- Channel management software subscription fees

**Common mistake:** Your Airbnb 1099-K reports *gross* receipts—before platform fees are deducted. You must separately deduct platform fees as an expense. If you just report the net deposits as income without deducting platform fees, you're overpaying.

### Cleaning and Turnover Costs

All professional cleaning between guest stays is 100% deductible:
- Cleaning service invoices
- Laundry and linen service
- Cleaning supplies purchased exclusively for the rental
- Damage restoration cleaning after guest stays

### Property Management Fees

If you use a property management company, their fee (typically 10–25% of gross revenue) is 100% deductible. For a host earning $80,000/year in rentals with a 15% management fee, that's $12,000 in deductible expenses.

### Guest Supplies and Amenities

Everything you provide exclusively for guests:
- Toiletries (shampoo, conditioner, body wash, lotion)
- Paper products (toilet paper, paper towels, tissues)
- Coffee, tea, sugar, cooking oils, and condiments stocked for guests
- Welcome basket items
- Beach/pool toys and inflatables
- Umbrellas, sunscreen, bug spray left for guests
- Local maps, guidebooks, and printed materials

Track these purchases separately from your household shopping. Over a full year of active hosting, these costs typically run $500–$4,000.

### Pest Control

Pest control treatments are 100% deductible as a direct property maintenance expense—particularly relevant for vacation properties near water or in humid climates.

### Reservation System and Software

Software subscriptions used to manage your rental:
- Property management systems (Guesty, Hostaway, Lodgify)
- Dynamic pricing tools (Beyond, PriceLabs, Wheelhouse)
- Channel management software
- STR compliance tools like [PermitPal](https://permitpalpro.com)
- Accounting software (QuickBooks, FreshBooks)

All fully deductible as business software expenses.

## Category C: Proportional Operating Expenses

These are deductible at the rental percentage (rental days ÷ total days used):

### Insurance

- STR-specific insurance premiums: full rental %
- Standard homeowner's insurance: rental %
- Umbrella liability policy (if applicable): rental %

You should have dedicated STR insurance, not just homeowner's insurance. Standard homeowner's policies typically exclude commercial activity. STR insurance is specifically designed for hosting and is fully deductible.

### Utilities

- Electricity, gas, and propane
- Water and sewer
- Trash and recycling collection
- Internet and cable/streaming services
- Telephone (landline or dedicated property phone)

All utilities at the rental percentage.

**Tip:** If you upgrade internet service to a high-speed commercial-grade connection specifically to serve guests, you may argue that the full cost is a rental expense—not just the rental percentage.

### HOA Dues

If your property is in an HOA, dues are deductible at the rental percentage. HOA special assessments for capital improvements must be depreciated, not expensed.

### General Maintenance and Repairs

Routine maintenance deductible at the rental percentage:
- HVAC annual servicing and filter replacements
- Pool cleaning service and chemicals
- Landscaping maintenance (not improvements)
- Gutter cleaning
- Exterior painting to maintain condition (not to upgrade)
- Plumbing maintenance
- Electrical maintenance

## Category D: Travel and Vehicle Expenses

### Mileage for Business Trips to the Property

If you travel to your rental property for business purposes, the mileage is deductible:
- Standard mileage rate: 67 cents/mile (2024 IRS rate; check current year rate)
- Qualifying purposes: repairs, inspections, meeting contractors, addressing compliance matters

**Essential:** Keep a contemporaneous mileage log with date, starting and ending point, business purpose, and miles. Reconstructed logs created at tax time are routinely disallowed in audits.

### Airfare and Long-Distance Travel

If your rental property requires air travel to reach, the airfare is deductible when traveling for qualifying business purposes. If you combine the business trip with personal vacation time, allocate the costs.

### Meals During Business Travel

50% of meal costs during legitimate business travel to or from the rental property are deductible.

## Category E: Professional Services

### CPA and Tax Preparation

The portion of your CPA's fees attributable to your rental property (Schedule E preparation, cost segregation analysis, rental-related planning) is fully deductible.

### Legal Fees

Attorney fees for:
- Reviewing rental agreements
- Permit applications and disputes
- Business entity formation
- Guest disputes or eviction proceedings
- Zoning and compliance matters

All 100% deductible rental business expenses.

### Property Inspectors

If you hire independent inspectors to verify property condition after guest stays or before new guests arrive, those inspection fees are deductible.

### Financial Advisors

Financial advisor fees specifically related to your rental portfolio and investment strategy are deductible.

## Category G: Startup Costs (First Year Only)

### Pre-Opening Expenses

In your first year of STR operation, you can deduct up to $5,000 in startup expenses immediately. Any amount above $5,000 is amortized over 180 months.

Qualifying startup costs:
- Market research expenses
- Initial advertising before the property opened for bookings
- Legal and accounting fees for setting up the business
- Travel to the property for pre-rental inspections
- Initial training courses on STR hosting

Document these carefully with dates—the IRS distinguishes between pre-opening startup costs (deductible/amortizable) and costs incurred after the property was already in service (regular operating expenses).

## Category H: Home Office Deduction

If you have a dedicated workspace in your primary home used exclusively and regularly for managing your rental business, you can deduct the proportional home costs.

**What qualifies:** A dedicated room or defined area used *only* for rental management. A corner of your living room doesn't qualify.

**What's deductible:**
- Proportional share of home mortgage interest or rent
- Proportional share of utilities
- Proportional share of home insurance
- Direct office expenses (furniture, equipment, supplies)

For hosts managing multiple properties, this deduction can be worth $3,000–$8,000 per year.

## Building Your Deduction Capture System

The best deductions are the ones you actually capture. Here's the system:

**Dedicated financial infrastructure:** Separate checking account and credit card for the rental business. All income deposits and all rental expenses flow through these accounts. Year-end reporting is then trivially easy.

**Receipt digitization:** Photograph every receipt immediately with your phone. Store in a dedicated cloud folder organized by tax category: Cleaning, Supplies, Platform Fees, Repairs, Utilities, etc.

**Day-by-day usage calendar:** Mark every day clearly: guest stay, personal use, or maintenance day. This calendar is the foundation of your rental percentage calculation—the single most important document in your tax file.

**Monthly reconciliation:** Spend 20 minutes per month reviewing transactions, confirming categorization, and noting anything unusual. Don't let it pile up until April.

**Annual review with CPA:** In December, do a pre-year-end review to identify any acceleration opportunities (bringing deductible expenses into the current year, making capital improvements before year-end for depreciation purposes).

## FAQ: Short-Term Rental Deductions

**Q: Can I deduct the cost of redecorating my rental property?**
A: Depends on whether it's a repair or improvement. Repainting walls to maintain existing condition = repair (deductible now). Replacing worn-out furniture with modern equivalents = deductible (replacement). Adding a new deck that wasn't there before = improvement (must be depreciated). Replacing the worn-out deck with a new one of the same size = repair (deductible now). The "betterment, restoration, or adaptation" test determines the distinction.

**Q: What if I use my rental property for Airbnb Experiences or other activities?**
A: If you host Airbnb Experiences (cooking classes, tours) at the rental property, that income is separate from rental income and generally treated as self-employment income on Schedule C. Expenses specifically related to the Experiences activity (supplies, ingredients, advertising) are deductible on Schedule C.

**Q: Can I deduct the cost of a new roof?**
A: A new roof is an improvement (not a repair) and must be capitalized and depreciated over 27.5 years (or 39 years if it's a commercial property). However, if the roof was damaged by a storm or casualty event, the replacement cost may be treated as a restoration repair—deductible in full in the year of replacement.

**Q: I purchased furniture specifically for the rental. Can I deduct it all this year?**
A: Generally, furniture is 5-year property under MACRS. Under the current 60% bonus depreciation rules (2026), you can deduct 60% of the cost in year one and depreciate the remaining 40% over 5 years. For items costing less than $2,500 (under the de minimis safe harbor), you may elect to expense them immediately.
